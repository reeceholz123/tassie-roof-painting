## 3. The positioning problem to solve first

Three sites in, the "family trade" pitch (brothers and a dad on roofs across generations) is the core differentiator. **Coast and Country quietly stretched that frame** — same owner Bryce, just operating under a different brand for the Hunter region.

If the Tassie business is **also Bryce's**, the family-trade story bends further. Two options:

**Option A — "Bryce expands south."** Position this as Bryce's Tasmania operation. About section reads something like: *"After three decades on roofs across NSW and beyond, Bryce now runs Coast and Country on the mainland and [Tassie brand] from [Tas base]. Same craft, same Dulux accreditation, same standard."* Honest, simple. Loses the multi-generational pitch but gains a "veteran tradie scaling deliberately" angle.

**Option B — "Local Tassie partner, family-tied."** A relative or long-time mate runs it on the ground in Tas, with Bryce backing the trade standards. About section: *"[Owner name] runs the Tassie crew. Trained alongside Bryce ([family link]) on jobs across NSW. Brought the same craft south when [reason]."* Keeps the family-trade pitch alive.

**Need the owner to clarify which is true** before writing About copy. Don't guess.

## 4. Tasmania-specific climate / region context

The brand language should reflect what Tassie roofs actually face, which is markedly different from NSW or QLD:

- **Cold, wet, lichen-prone.** Damp southerly weather, persistent moisture, heavy lichen on south-facing tiles.
- **Roaring Forties wind exposure** on west coast and Bass Strait properties.
- **Snow at altitude** (Central Highlands, Cradle Mountain region) — coatings need freeze/thaw tolerance.
- **Strong heritage stock** — sandstone cottages, weatherboard farmhouses, slate and corrugated iron rural roofs.
- **Salt air** on every coast (the island is mostly coast).

Likely service emphasis: **soft wash and moss treatment will be much more prominent** than in QLD. Heat-reflective coatings less so. Heritage-property work probably bigger than in any other site.

**Region split — strong default to suggest:**
- **South** — Hobart, Kingston, Sandy Bay, Glenorchy, Sorell, New Norfolk, Huonville, Margate
- **North** — Launceston, Devonport, Burnie, Deloraine, George Town, Westbury, Beaconsfield, Scottsdale
- **East / North-West coast** (or split into two) — depends where the business is based

## 5. Visual identity directions (must distinguish from the other three)

The other three sites have claimed:
- Rob's: red-orange + Anton/Oswald/Montserrat
- Heatshield: red + Anton/Oswald/Montserrat
- Coast and Country: deep navy + bright blue + Big Shoulders Display / Archivo Narrow / Source Sans 3

**The Tassie site needs its own pair.** Three palette directions worth proposing:

- **A — Highland heritage:** Forest green `#2F4A38` + warm copper `#B47A3A`. Reads slate-roof, sandstone, deep-bush.
- **B — Bass Strait industrial:** Charcoal-blue `#2C3E4A` + ochre `#D49B3A`. Cooler, more graphic, leans west-coast.
- **C — Heritage maritime:** Deep teal-green `#1F4544` + cream `#E8DDC0` as a true second neutral, with a single warm red accent `#A53826` for CTAs. Most editorial, hardest to execute, highest payoff.

**Font stack directions** (must differ from both the Anton/Oswald/Montserrat set AND the Big Shoulders/Archivo Narrow/Source Sans 3 set):

- **Stack A — heritage-craft:** `Antonio` (display, condensed slab-y) + `Saira Condensed` (UI) + `DM Sans` (body)
- **Stack B — sharper-modern:** `Bebas Neue` (display) + `Barlow Condensed` (UI) + `Inter` (body)
- **Stack C — editorial-warm:** `Oswald` is taken; alternative — `Teko` (display) + `Barlow Semi Condensed` (UI) + `IBM Plex Sans` (body)
